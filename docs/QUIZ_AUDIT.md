## Editorial bar

1. Verify one unambiguously correct answer against the lesson's model and assumptions.
2. Ask for a calculation, prediction, or distinction when the concept supports it. Reserve direct recall for a small number of foundation questions.
3. Make each distractor a plausible mistake, such as confusing unilateral with joint gains, maximum with maximal, or an ex ante signal with the signal conditional on winning.
4. Keep options comparable in length and specificity. Put the explanation in feedback.
5. Explain why each wrong option fails, and keep the feedback consistent with its current wording.
6. Rotate review topics across prior lessons. A review should retrieve a useful earlier idea, not repeat the same definition at every opening.

This pass shortened more than 160 correct options, rewrote both distractors and their feedback in 15 end questions, and replaced 13 repetitive opening review prompts. The remaining questions passed a structural check for one correct option each. Future lesson edits should use the bar above as an authoring check.
