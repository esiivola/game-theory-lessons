# Dominant and dominated strategies

<p class="lead">A strategy is <span class="in-acc">dominant</span> when it beats your alternatives against every move the other player could make. Its opposite, a strictly <span class="in-def">dominated</span> strategy, should never be played, and spotting one shrinks the game.</p>

## Find the move that always wins

<PayoffMatrix
  client:visible
  mode="read"
  exhibit="Exhibit 1"
  caption="Profit (you, them)"
  rows={[{ key: 'A', label: 'You advertise' }, { key: 'N', label: 'You do not' }]}
  cols={[{ key: 'A', label: 'They advertise' }, { key: 'N', label: 'They do not' }]}
  payoffs={{ A: { A: [3, 3], N: [6, 1] }, N: { A: [1, 6], N: [4, 4] } }}
/>

Read down your two rows. If they advertise, you get 3 from advertising versus 1 from not. If they do not, you get 6 versus 4. Advertising wins in both columns.

<WorkedExample title="Eliminate the dominated move">
For you, advertising beats not-advertising in every column ($3>1$ and $6>4$), so "do not advertise" is strictly dominated and can be crossed out. By symmetry the same holds for them. What remains is a single cell, $(\text{advertise}, \text{advertise})$, paying $(3,3)$, even though both would prefer the $(4,4)$ they reach by staying quiet. Sound familiar? That tension returns in the next unit.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">If one of your moves is at least as good against everything and strictly better against something, take it and never look back. If a move is dominated, delete it.</Callout>
</Section>
