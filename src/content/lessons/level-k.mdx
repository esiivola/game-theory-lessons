# Limited reasoning: level-k

<p class="lead">Equilibrium assumes reasoning goes all the way down: everyone is rational, knows others are, and so on without end. Real people truncate. Level-k models say each person best-responds to a belief that others reason one step less than they do, and that simple idea fits laboratory play remarkably well.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Getting to 0 requires believing everyone reasons infinitely far, and that they believe it too. Real crowds do not. Most people take one or two steps: "if others guess randomly (average 50), I should say 33; if they say 33, I should say 22." Then they stop. Knowing the crowd stops early, the winning guess is not 0 but a bit below the average the crowd actually produces. Modeling the distribution of reasoning depths, rather than assuming it is infinite, is what predicts real behaviour.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="level-k and cognitive hierarchy">
In a level-k model, a level-0 player acts non-strategically (here, guesses uniformly, averaging 50). A level-$k$ player best-responds to the belief that everyone else is level-$(k-1)$. In the beauty contest, level-1 guesses $\tfrac23 \cdot 50 = 33$, level-2 guesses $\tfrac23 \cdot 33 = 22$, and so on. The cognitive-hierarchy variant assumes a level-$k$ player best-responds to a mix of all lower levels, with the depth distribution often Poisson with mean around $1.5$.
</DefinitionAside>

Full rationality iterates the two-thirds map to the fixed point $0$. Empirically the modal guesses are $33$ and $22$, exactly one and two steps down from $50$ (Nagel, 1995). Because the crowd is a mixture of shallow reasoners, the average lands well above $0$, and the best response to that crowd, around $20$ to $22$, wins. The equilibrium prediction of $0$ is not just wrong; it is a losing guess.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Climb the ladder, then stop early">
Level-0 guesses average $50$. A level-1 reasoner best-responds: $\tfrac23 \cdot 50 = 33$. A level-2 reasoner assumes others are level-1 and guesses $\tfrac23 \cdot 33 = 22$. Level-3 would guess $\tfrac23 \cdot 22 \approx 15$. Iterating forever reaches $0$, the Nash equilibrium.

But suppose the crowd is mostly level-1 and level-2, with a few level-0 and level-3. The average of their guesses is roughly in the low $30$s, so two-thirds of it is around $20$ to $22$. Best-responding to the actual crowd, not to an imagined infinitely-deep one, you should guess about $20$. Playing $0$ assumes everyone else reasoned to the limit, which they did not, so it loses.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">People reason a bounded number of steps, not to infinity. Level-k and cognitive-hierarchy models best-respond to shallow-reasoning crowds and predict real play, where the equilibrium of 0 both mispredicts and loses.</Callout>
</Section>
