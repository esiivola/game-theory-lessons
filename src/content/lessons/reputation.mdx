# Reputation

<p class="lead">Backward induction said a threat to fight entry is empty: once entry happens, fighting only hurts you. That logic breaks when rivals are not sure who they are dealing with. A sliver of doubt that you might be the type who always fights, defended by fighting early, can deter challengers for a very long time.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
A normal incumbent would rather accommodate any single entrant. But accommodating once tells every future entrant that it is normal, and they all pour in. Fighting, by contrast, keeps them guessing and keeps them out. As long as there are enough towns left, the future deterrence is worth more than the one-time cost of a fight, so even a normal firm fights, and the small chance it is genuinely tough is enough to make entrants believe the fight will come. The doubt is the weapon.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

Let the incumbent be **tough** with small prior probability $\mu$ (it always fights, perhaps because fighting is how it is wired), and **normal** otherwise (payoffs as in the entry game: accommodating an entrant beats fighting in a single town). There are $N$ towns.

Solve from the end. In the **last** town the normal incumbent has no future to protect, so it accommodates; entrants there can safely enter. But **earlier**, a normal incumbent compares two paths. Accommodate now: gain $1$ this town, but reveal normality and face entry in every remaining town. Fight now: lose the one-period cost, but keep entrants out for many towns to come. When enough towns remain, the deterrence value swamps the cost, so the normal type fights.

<DefinitionAside label="reputation effect (KMRW)">
With a small probability that a long-lived player is a committed type, the normal type mimics that type to build a reputation. In the chain-store game, a tiny doubt about toughness supports deterrence across almost all towns; the reputation only unravels in the final rounds, where mimicry no longer pays. Entrants stay out precisely because they cannot tell a tough incumbent from a normal one defending its name.
</DefinitionAside>

This resolves **Selten's chain-store paradox**. Under complete information, backward induction says the incumbent cannot deter anyone: fighting is never credible, so entry occurs everywhere. Add an ounce of incomplete information and deterrence returns across the whole chain. The paradox was an artifact of assuming the type was known.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Why a normal incumbent fights early">
Take the entrant payoffs: accommodate gives the entrant $+2$, a fight gives $-1$, staying out gives $0$. An entrant enters only if the expected payoff beats $0$, that is, if a fight is unlikely enough.

**Last town.** The normal incumbent accommodates (no future to protect). Knowing this, and that the incumbent is tough only with small probability, entry pays, so entrants enter.

**Earlier towns.** If the normal incumbent ever accommodates, it is revealed normal and every later entrant enters, costing it the monopoly profit in all those towns. Fighting instead preserves the doubt and keeps entrants out. With many towns left, the preserved profits exceed the one-period cost of fighting (accommodate $1$ versus fight $0$), so the normal type fights. Because both the tough and normal types then fight, an entrant who sees fights cannot rule out toughness, so the expected payoff to entering stays negative and they stay out. Deterrence holds until the horizon comes into view.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">A small doubt about your type, worth defending, lets you build a reputation that deters rivals. It holds across a long horizon and cracks only near the end, when protecting the reputation no longer pays.</Callout>
</Section>
