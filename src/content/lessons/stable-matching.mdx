# Stable matching

<p class="lead">Match two sides with preferences, medical residents to hospitals, students to schools, and you want stability: no pair who would both prefer each other to their assigned partners. Deferred acceptance always reaches a stable matching, and it quietly favours whichever side does the proposing.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Proposers get to try their top choice first and only move down when rejected, so they end up as high on their own list as stability allows. Receivers can only accept or reject what comes to them, so they end up as low as stability allows. The result is stable either way: no pair wants to defect. But the two runs produce different stable matchings, one best for each side, which is why the question of who proposes is a real design choice, not a technicality.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="deferred acceptance and stability">
A matching is stable if no unmatched pair prefer each other to their assigned partners (no blocking pair). In deferred acceptance, each member of the proposing side proposes down its list; each receiver tentatively holds its best proposer so far and rejects the rest; rejected proposers try their next choice. The process ends with a stable matching, and it is proposer-optimal: every proposer gets the best partner it could have in any stable matching, and every receiver the worst.
</DefinitionAside>

In the cyclic instance, man-proposing round 1 has $m_1 \to w_1$, $m_2 \to w_2$, $m_3 \to w_3$, and every woman receives exactly one proposal, so all are held and the algorithm stops. Each man gets his first choice; each woman ($w_1$ ranks $m_2 > m_3 > m_1$, and so on) gets her last. Checking stability: every man already has his top choice, so no man would leave, and therefore no blocking pair exists. Woman-proposing produces the mirror image, best for the women.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Run it, then check for blocking pairs">
Man-proposing: in round 1 each man proposes to his first choice, $m_1 \to w_1$, $m_2 \to w_2$, $m_3 \to w_3$. No woman gets two proposals, so each holds hers and the algorithm ends immediately. Every man has his first choice; every woman her last.

Now hunt for a blocking pair. Take $w_1$, who got $m_1$ (her least favourite) and would prefer $m_2$. But $m_2$ has $w_2$, his first choice, and prefers her to $w_1$. So $(m_2, w_1)$ is not a blocking pair. The same holds everywhere: every man has his top choice and will not stray. No blocking pair exists, so the matching is stable, even though every woman is matched to her worst option.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Deferred acceptance always yields a stable matching, and it is best for the proposing side and worst for the receiving side. Stability does not pin down a unique matching, so who proposes is a genuine design decision.</Callout>
</Section>
