# Evolutionarily stable strategies

<p class="lead">Nash equilibrium assumes reasoning. Evolution needs none: strategies that do well reproduce, those that do badly die out. An evolutionarily stable strategy is one that, once common, resists invasion by any rare mutant. In Hawk-Dove it is a precise mix of aggression and restraint.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
The mix is self-correcting. When Hawks are scarce, they mostly meet Doves and grab the resource uncontested, so they thrive and their share rises. When Hawks are common, they mostly meet other Hawks and pay the injury cost, so they do worse than Doves and their share falls. The only fraction where neither type has an edge, so neither grows, is $V/C$. No animal is reasoning; the arithmetic of reproduction does the work.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="evolutionarily stable strategy">
A strategy is an ESS if, when almost everyone plays it, no rare mutant playing anything else can earn higher average fitness and invade. Formally $s$ is an ESS if for every alternative $s'$, either $u(s,s) > u(s',s)$, or $u(s,s) = u(s',s)$ and $u(s,s') > u(s',s')$. Every ESS is a Nash equilibrium, but the invasion condition is strictly stronger.
</DefinitionAside>

Hawk-Dove payoffs: two Hawks split the resource and share the injury, $(V-C)/2$; a Hawk against a Dove takes it all, $V$; a Dove against a Hawk gets nothing; two Doves share peacefully, $V/2$. With $V = 2$, $C = 6$, that is Hawk-Hawk $= -2$, Hawk-Dove $= 2$, Dove-Hawk $= 0$, Dove-Dove $= 1$.

When $C > V$ there is no pure ESS: all-Hawk is invadable by Doves (Hawks injure each other), and all-Dove is invadable by Hawks (a lone Hawk cleans up). The stable mix sets Hawk and Dove fitness equal. At Hawk fraction $p$, Hawk fitness is $2 - 4p$ and Dove fitness is $1 - p$; equating gives $p^* = V/C = 1/3$.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Find the stable Hawk fraction">
At a Hawk fraction $p$, a Hawk meets a Hawk with probability $p$ (payoff $-2$) and a Dove with probability $1-p$ (payoff $2$), so its fitness is $-2p + 2(1-p) = 2 - 4p$. A Dove earns $0$ against Hawks and $1$ against Doves, so its fitness is $1 - p$.

Set them equal: $2 - 4p = 1 - p$, giving $3p = 1$, so $p^* = 1/3 = V/C$. Below $1/3$, Hawks earn more and their share grows; above $1/3$, they earn less and it shrinks. So $1/3$ is stable against invasion from either side, exactly the ESS. Raising the injury cost $C$ lowers the stable Hawk share, since fighting becomes more dangerous.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">An evolutionarily stable strategy resists invasion by any rare mutant, a stronger test than Nash. In Hawk-Dove with costly injuries, the stable population is a mix, with a Hawk fraction of exactly V/C.</Callout>
</Section>
