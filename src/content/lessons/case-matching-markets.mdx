# Matching markets in the wild

<p class="lead">Deferred acceptance is not a toy. The National Resident Matching Program and school-choice systems use it or close variants. Kidney exchange instead uses cycles and chains. This case shows the theory doing real work: matching rules can supply properties that decentralized markets do not.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Left alone, a matching market unravels: if any doctor-hospital pair prefer each other to their assigned partners, they cut a private deal, so hospitals rush to lock in doctors ever earlier with exploding offers. A stable matching removes every such incentive, because no blocking pair exists by construction. Deferred acceptance computes one for everyone simultaneously, replacing a scramble with a single clean assignment. That is why real clearinghouses adopted it.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The real episodes

<DefinitionAside label="matching in practice">
The National Resident Matching Program adopted a deferred-acceptance algorithm (redesigned by Roth and Peranson in 1998) and matches tens of thousands of doctors yearly with almost no unraveling. Boston switched its school assignment to a strategy-proof deferred-acceptance mechanism in 2005, so families could rank honestly. Kidney exchange (Roth, Sonmez and Unver) uses top-trading-cycles ideas: patients with willing but incompatible donors swap donors around cycles and chains, unlocking transplants that no single pair could achieve.
</DefinitionAside>

Two design lessons recur. First, stability prevents the unraveling that plagued unregulated markets. Second, strategy-proofness matters: the old Boston mechanism punished honest ranking, so sophisticated families gamed it and naive ones lost out. Deferred acceptance lets everyone report truthfully, which is fairer and simpler. Kidney exchange adds the twist that a non-directed donor can start a long chain, relaxing the mutual-compatibility bottleneck of a simple two-way swap.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Stability is what markets could not self-provide">
Run the match: doctors propose, hospitals hold their favourite so far and reject the rest, rejected doctors try their next choice, and the process settles at a stable assignment with no blocking pair. Every doctor is as well off as any stable matching allows, and no hospital-doctor pair would both rather defect.

Now imagine no clearinghouse. A hospital that fears losing a top doctor makes an exploding offer months early; doctors accept out of fear; the market moves earlier each year until it breaks. This is exactly the instability that a stable matching rules out. In medical matching and school choice, deferred acceptance supplies stability and safe truthful ranking for the proposing side. Kidney exchange uses a different cycle-and-chain design to find compatible transplants.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Real matching markets can unravel without a clearinghouse. Deferred acceptance supplies stable assignments and safe truthful ranking for the proposing side. Top-trading-cycles ideas supply efficient exchanges of owned goods, and kidney exchange adapts cycles and chains to medical compatibility.</Callout>
</Section>
