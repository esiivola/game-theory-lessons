# Learning and adaptation

<p class="lead">Fictitious play was one learning rule; reinforcement learning is another. Experience-weighted attraction unifies them. Two parameters, one for memory and one for how much you learn from options you did not take, span the whole space, and fitting them to data lands squarely between the two classic extremes.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Reinforcement updates only the action you actually chose, so you learn about the road not taken only by eventually trying it, which is slow. Belief learning imagines the payoff every action would have given and updates them all, so it corrects course quickly. EWA puts a dial (imagination) between these: turn it to zero for reinforcement, to one for belief learning. Fitting the dial to human data lands around a half, meaning people do weigh what they missed, but not as heavily as what they experienced.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="experience-weighted attraction">
EWA maintains an attraction $A_j$ for each action, updated as $A_j(t) = \dfrac{\phi\,N(t-1)\,A_j(t-1) + [\delta + (1-\delta)\mathbf{1}\{j\text{ played}\}]\,\pi_j(t)}{N(t)}$, with $N(t) = \rho\,N(t-1) + 1$, and choice by logit over attractions. The dial $\delta$ is imagination: $\delta = 0$ updates only the played action (reinforcement), while $\delta = 1$ with $\phi = \rho$ updates every action by its foregone payoff (belief learning / fictitious play). The dial $\phi$ is memory.
</DefinitionAside>

Setting $\delta = 0$ recovers reinforcement learning: the attraction of an unchosen action is unchanged, so you only learn by doing. Setting $\delta = 1$ with $\phi = \rho$ recovers belief learning: every action is credited with its counterfactual payoff, exactly fictitious play. Estimates from experiments put $\phi \approx 0.8$ to $1$ and $\delta \approx 0.5$: people remember most of the past and weigh foregone payoffs about half as much as realized ones. One model, tuned by data, subsumes both traditions.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Turn the imagination dial">
Suppose action A pays $6$ and action B would have paid $4$ this round, and you played A. Under $\delta = 0$ (reinforcement), only A's attraction rises; B's is untouched, so you learn nothing about B unless you try it. Under $\delta = 1$ (belief learning), both attractions update: A by $6$, B by $4$, so you immediately compare the two and lean toward A.

The reinforcement learner may take many rounds to discover B is worse (or A is better than some third option), because it only updates what it samples. The belief learner corrects in one round. EWA's $\delta \approx 0.5$ estimate says people do learn from foregone payoffs, roughly half-weight, so they adapt faster than pure reinforcement but slower than full belief learning.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Experience-weighted attraction nests reinforcement and belief learning in one model with two dials, memory and imagination. Fit to data, people weigh foregone payoffs about half as much as realized ones.</Callout>
</Section>
