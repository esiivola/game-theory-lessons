# Quantal response equilibrium

<p class="lead">Level-k relaxed how deeply players reason. Quantal response relaxes how sharply they act. Instead of always best-responding, players pick better actions with higher probability, and equilibrium is where these noisy choices are mutually consistent. A single rationality parameter tunes it from random to Nash.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
People are payoff-sensitive but noisy: a better option is more attractive, so it is chosen more, but small mistakes remain. The rationality parameter measures how sensitive: at zero, payoffs are ignored and choice is a coin flip; as it grows, the better action dominates, and in the limit you recover exact best response. And beliefs stay consistent: each player's noisy choices are a best noisy-response to the others', so this is still an equilibrium, just a smoothed one.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="quantal response equilibrium">
In a logit QRE, a player chooses action $a$ with probability proportional to $\exp(\lambda \cdot EU(a))$, where $EU(a)$ is its expected payoff against the other players' choice probabilities and $\lambda$ is the precision parameter. Equilibrium requires all of these noisy responses to be mutually consistent. At $\lambda = 0$ all actions are equally likely. As precision grows, limit points are Nash equilibria, though games can have more than one QRE branch.
</DefinitionAside>

For two actions with expected-payoff difference $d = EU(A) - EU(B)$, the probability of $A$ is

$$
P(A) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\lambda d}}.
$$

This formula is one player's logit response, not yet a QRE. In the displayed coordination game, if the other player chooses A with probability $q$, then $d=2q-(1-q)=3q-1$. A symmetric QRE solves the fixed-point equation

$$
p = \frac{1}{1+e^{-\lambda(3p-1)}}.
$$

The game solves this consistency condition for both players as the precision slider moves.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Close the loop between two noisy responses">
At $\lambda=0$, every payoff is multiplied by zero, so both players choose A with probability $1/2$. At a positive $\lambda$, start from a conjecture $q$ about the other player's probability of A. Your payoff advantage is $3q-1$, so your logit response is $1/(1+e^{-\lambda(3q-1)})$.

Equilibrium requires your resulting probability to equal the probability used in the other player's calculation. Solving those two response equations together gives the probabilities shown in the game. Raising $\lambda$ makes choices more sensitive to expected payoff and moves the displayed branch toward the payoff-dominant Nash equilibrium $(A,A)$.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Quantal response equilibrium replaces perfect best response with noisy, payoff-sensitive choice governed by one rationality dial. It spans random play to Nash and fits real, mistake-prone behaviour in between.</Callout>
</Section>
