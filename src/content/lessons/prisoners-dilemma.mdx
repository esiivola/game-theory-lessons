# The Prisoner's Dilemma

<p class="lead">Two gas stations sit across the street from each other. Each morning each owner makes one choice: <span class="in-coop">hold prices</span>, or <span class="in-def">cut them</span> to pull in the other's customers. If both hold, both do well. If one cuts while the other holds, the cutter takes the street.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Cutting pays more for you whatever the other owner does. If they hold, cutting wins the street. If they cut, you cannot afford to be the only one holding. So both cut, and both earn less than if they had left prices alone. Neither owner is careless. They are stuck in the logic of the situation.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

Write the game in normal form as $G=(N,(S_i),(u_i))$: players $N=\{1,2\}$, a strategy set $S_i=\{C,D\}$ for each (hold or cut), and a payoff $u_i$ for every pair of choices.

Label the four payoffs with the usual names: $R$ for both holding (reward), $T$ for cutting against a holder (temptation), $P$ for both cutting (punishment), $S$ for holding against a cutter (sucker). Here $R=3,\ T=5,\ P=1,\ S=0$, which meet the two conditions that make it a Prisoner's Dilemma:

$$
T > R > P > S \quad\text{and}\quad 2R > T + S.
$$

Cutting strictly dominates holding, since the cutter does better against either move ($5>3$ and $1>0$):

$$
u_i(D, a) > u_i(C, a) \quad \forall\, a.
$$

<DefinitionAside label="Nash equilibrium">
A profile $s^*$ is a Nash equilibrium if $u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}^*) \ge u_i(s_i, s_{-i}^*)$ for every $s_i$ and every $i$.
</DefinitionAside>

A strictly dominant strategy is played in every equilibrium, so $(D,D)$ is the only one, paying $(1,1)$. But $(C,C)$ pays $(3,3)$ and beats it for both players. The equilibrium is the outcome nobody wanted.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Find the equilibrium yourself

<WorkedExample title="Show that both-cut is the only equilibrium">
Check each player's best reply. If they **hold**, you get $3$ from holding but $5$ from cutting, so you cut. If they **cut**, you get $0$ from holding but $1$ from cutting, so you cut again. Cutting is your best reply to everything, and the same is true for them. The only cell where neither wants to move is $(\text{cut}, \text{cut})$, paying $(1,1)$.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Each player, acting sensibly alone, drives both to the outcome they were trying to avoid.</Callout>
</Section>
