# Learning in games

<p class="lead">Equilibrium is usually justified by assuming players are hyper-rational. Learning offers a humbler story: players track what opponents have done and best-respond to the record. In zero-sum and potential games, this fictitious play converges, and no-regret learning reaches equilibrium concepts more generally.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
The bot is not clever, just observant: it counts your moves and flees the one you favour. So any lean you develop gets exploited, and the only play it cannot beat is an even mix. Meanwhile the bot's own moves respond to your history, and when both sides learn this way, their empirical frequencies chase each other around and settle, on average, at the 50/50 equilibrium. The equilibrium emerges from adaptation, not calculation.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

<DefinitionAside label="fictitious play and no-regret">
In fictitious play, each player forms a belief equal to the empirical frequency of the opponent's past moves and best-responds to it. In zero-sum games and in potential games, the empirical frequencies of play converge to a Nash equilibrium. No-regret (or regret-matching) learning is more general: if each player's average regret for not having played any fixed action goes to zero, the empirical joint distribution converges to the set of coarse correlated equilibria.
</DefinitionAside>

In Matching Pennies the matcher wants to match, the mismatcher to differ. Each best-responds to the other's running Heads frequency. The per-round beliefs cycle, but the time-average of each player's own play converges to $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$, the mixed equilibrium. So a player who never heard of mixed strategies nonetheless plays one on average.

Fictitious play does not converge in every game (it can cycle in some), but it does in the two big classes above. No-regret dynamics guarantee, in any game, average play within $O(\sqrt{\ln n / T})$ of the best fixed action, which pins the long-run behaviour to equilibrium sets even when full convergence fails.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Why an even mix is the only safe play">
Suppose you have played Heads 6 times and Tails 4. The bot believes you play Heads with frequency $0.6$ and, as the mismatcher, plays Tails to avoid you, so you lose when you play Heads. Lean the other way and it flips to Heads. Any imbalance in your record hands the bot a profitable response.

The only frequency it cannot exploit is $0.5$: at an even, patternless mix, its Heads and Tails responses both break even against you. So your best long-run play is $50/50$, and the bot's belief converges to that $50/50$ frequency. Both sides' empirical play settles at the mixed equilibrium, reached by adaptation rather than by solving the indifference equation directly.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Simple learners who best-respond to the past can converge on equilibrium without computing it: fictitious play reaches Nash in zero-sum and potential games, and no-regret learning reaches correlated equilibria in general.</Callout>
</Section>
