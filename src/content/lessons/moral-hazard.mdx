# Contract theory and moral hazard

<p class="lead">Moral hazard is a hidden-action problem: the agent chooses effort you cannot see, and only a noisy outcome is observable. To motivate effort you must make pay depend on the outcome, which hands the agent a reward for good luck. That extra pay, the rent, is the price of not being able to watch.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
Because effort is hidden, a flat wage gives the agent no reason to bear the cost of working, so they shirk. The only lever you have is the outcome, so pay must rise with high output. But output is noisy: a hard worker sometimes gets Low and a shirker sometimes gets High. To make effort the agent's best choice, the bonus has to be big enough that the extra chance of the bonus outweighs the effort cost, and that same bonus overpays the agent on average, leaving them a rent. You cannot separate rewarding effort from rewarding luck.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

Output is $100$ (High) or $0$ (Low). Effort $e \in \{0,1\}$ costs $15$ when $e=1$; $\Pr(\text{High}\mid 1) = 0.8$ and $\Pr(\text{High}\mid 0) = 0.4$. The agent is risk-neutral with limited liability (wages $\ge 0$) and reservation utility $0$. Write the contract as a base $w_L$ and a high-output wage $w_H$.

**Incentive compatibility.** The agent picks $e=1$ over $e=0$ iff

$$
0.8\,w_H + 0.2\,w_L - 15 \ \ge\ 0.4\,w_H + 0.6\,w_L,
$$

which simplifies to $w_H - w_L \ge 37.5$. The bonus, not the base, drives effort.

**Cheapest incentive contract.** With limited liability set $w_L = 0$, so $w_H = 37.5$. The agent's expected utility is $0.8(37.5) - 15 = 15 > 0$, so participation (IR) is slack: the agent earns a **rent** of $15$. The principal's profit is $0.8(100) - 0.8(37.5) = 50$, above the $40$ from paying nothing and letting the agent shirk.

<DefinitionAside label="moral hazard and the rent">
When actions are hidden, pay must be tied to observable output to satisfy incentive compatibility, which exposes a risk-averse or limited-liability agent to outcome risk and generally leaves them a rent. With observable effort (the first best) the principal would pay just the effort cost $15$ and keep $65$. The hidden-action loss here is $65 - 50 = 15$, exactly the rent the agent captures.
</DefinitionAside>
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Find the profit-maximising contract">
Start with incentive compatibility. Effort raises the chance of High by $0.8 - 0.4 = 0.4$, so the expected gain from effort is $0.4(w_H - w_L)$, and the agent works only if this covers the cost: $0.4(w_H - w_L) \ge 15$, i.e., $w_H - w_L \ge 37.5$.

Make the spread as cheap as possible under limited liability: $w_L = 0$, $w_H = 37.5$. Check participation: expected pay is $0.8(37.5) = 30$, minus effort $15$, leaves $15 \ge 0$, so the agent accepts and pockets a rent of $15$. Principal profit is expected output minus expected wage, $80 - 30 = 50$. Inducing no effort would pay $0$ and earn $0.4(100) = 40$, so motivating effort is better. The best contract is $(0, 37.5)$, and the $15$ rent is the cost of the hidden action.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Hidden effort forces you to pay for output, not effort, which rewards luck and leaves the agent a rent. A flat salary kills incentives; the fix is the smallest bonus that makes working worthwhile, and its rent is the price of not being able to watch.</Callout>
</Section>
