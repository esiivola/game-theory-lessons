# What is a game?

<p class="lead">A game is three things: the <span class="in-acc">players</span>, the <span class="in-acc">strategies</span> each can choose, and the <span class="in-acc">payoffs</span> they get for every combination. A payoff table lets you read all of it at a glance.</p>

<Section id="pieces">
## The pieces, precisely

A game in normal (strategic) form is a tuple $G = (N, (S_i)_{i \in N}, (u_i)_{i \in N})$: a set of players $N$, a set of strategies $S_i$ for each player, and a payoff function $u_i$ that assigns a number to every combination of choices.

A choice by everyone at once is a strategy profile $s = (s_i, s_{-i})$, where $s_{-i}$ is shorthand for what everyone except $i$ does. Payoffs are utilities, not dollars: they rank outcomes, and only the order matters.

<DefinitionAside label="strategy profile">
A profile $s$ lists one strategy per player. Player $i$ evaluates it through $u_i(s)$, holding fixed what the others, $s_{-i}$, chose.
</DefinitionAside>
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="Read a cell, then read a row">
Start with one cell. Pick the row for your choice and the column for theirs, and read the pair. Row "you open early", column "they open late" gives $(3, 1)$: the first number is always yours, so you make $3$ and they make $1$.

Now compare a whole row against the other, holding their choice fixed. Suppose they open late. Your early row pays $3$, your late row pays $2$, so against a late rival you want to open early. Fix their choice the other way, at early: now your early row pays $0$ and your late row pays $1$, so you would rather open late. Comparing down a column with their choice held fixed is the move you will use in every lesson from here.

**Faded.** Do the same for them. If you open early, is their better reply early or late? Read their numbers (the second in each pair) along the "you open early" row: early pays them $0$, late pays them $1$, so they open late.

**Solo.** There is no row that wins for you in both columns. Say in one sentence what that means for how you should choose.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">In a game, the best move is not a property of you alone. It is a response to what everyone else is doing.</Callout>
