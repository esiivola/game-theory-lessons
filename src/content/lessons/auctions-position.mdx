# Auctions III: position auctions

<p class="lead">Selling one item is one thing; selling ranked slots that each deliver different traffic is another. Position auctions allocate ad slots to advertisers. VCG would make truthful bidding dominant, but the industry standard, the generalized second price, does not, though its stable outcome lands in the same place.</p>

<Section id="why">
## Why did that happen?

<Callout kind="ask">
VCG charges you the harm you do to others, a number independent of your own bid, so you have no reason to lie. GSP charges you the bid just below yours, which depends on where everyone sits. Under truthful bids GSP overcharges the top slot compared with VCG, so a truthful advertiser would rather shade or even prefer a cheaper lower slot. Advertisers adjust until no one envies another's slot-and-price, and that envy-free resting point happens to reproduce the VCG payments, even though the rule itself is not truthful.
</Callout>
</Section>

<Section id="math">
## The math

Slots deliver $100$ and $50$ clicks; advertisers $A$, $B$, $C$ value a click at $10$, $6$, $4$. The efficient assignment pairs the highest value with the most clicks: $A \to$ slot 1, $B \to$ slot 2, welfare $10(100) + 6(50) = 1300$.

**VCG payments** charge each winner its externality. For $A$: without $A$, the others do best with $B \to 100$, $C \to 50$, welfare $800$; with $A$ present, the others get only $B \to 50 = 300$. So $A$ pays $800 - 300 = 500$ (5 per click). For $B$: without $B$, others get $A \to 100$, $C \to 50 = 1200$; with $B$, others get $A \to 100 = 1000$; $B$ pays $200$ (4 per click).

<DefinitionAside label="GSP versus VCG">
The generalized second-price auction ranks advertisers by bid and charges each the next advertiser's bid per click. With one slot it is the ordinary second-price auction and is truthful. With several slots it is no longer truthful: a bidder can gain by shading, or by preferring a lower slot. Its lowest envy-free equilibrium, however, yields exactly the VCG payments, so the efficient assignment and VCG-level prices emerge despite the non-truthful rule.
</DefinitionAside>

Under truthful bids GSP charges $A$ the next bid $6$ over $100$ clicks, so $600 > 500$. That overcharge is why truth-telling is not an equilibrium under GSP.
</Section>

<Section id="worked">
## Work it out

<WorkedExample title="VCG payments, then the GSP gap">
Compute $A$'s VCG payment as displaced welfare. Others' best welfare without $A$: give slot 1 to $B$ (600) and slot 2 to $C$ (200), total $800$. Others' welfare when $A$ takes slot 1: only $B$ in slot 2, $300$. Payment $= 800 - 300 = 500$. Likewise $B$ pays $1200 - 1000 = 200$.

Now GSP under truthful bids: slot 1's price is the next bid, $B = 6$, times slot 1's $100$ clicks, so $600$. That exceeds $A$'s VCG payment of $500$. Facing that, $A$ would shade its bid or reconsider the slot, so truthful bidding is not stable. The envy-free equilibrium of GSP settles at bids that make each advertiser content with its slot and price, reproducing the VCG numbers.
</WorkedExample>
</Section>

<Section id="key">
## Key idea

<Callout kind="key-idea">Position auctions rank advertisers and charge by slot. VCG makes honesty dominant by pricing externalities; the widely used generalized second price is not truthful, but its stable, envy-free outcome matches VCG all the same.</Callout>
</Section>
